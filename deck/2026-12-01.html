DiscoveryDec 1

COL Huston's feedback

Open architecture

Don't depend on specific sensors or models. Swap or add parts without a redesign.

Track custody

When a drone leaves camera or radar view, acoustic sensors keep the track.

Single track

The operator needs the same drone on one track, regardless of which sensor reports it.

Prototype scope

Exact accuracy is not the goal; parts can be simulated.

Design rationale

Why each part was chosen and how it would be improved.

Priority

Track continuity across sensors. The other limits depend on it.

MVP & testingDec 1

Localization

Timing

LoRa cannot sync nodes closely enough to time sound arrival, so position comes from how loud each node hears it.

Method

Each node reports loudness. Node positions are known. Louder node = drone closer to it.

Nodes

8 per building: street level and roof at every corner. Demo: 4 nodes on 2 corners.

Every report carries loudness, confidence and rotor pitch.

MVP & testingDec 1

Node pipeline

1Listen4 mics combined for a stronger signal
2CleanFilter wind rumble and hum
3FFTSound into frequencies, 15 per second
4CombEvenly spaced peaks: the rotor signature
5ConfirmPattern holds over several frames
6AlertPitch, loudness, confidence over LoRa

Generator: fixed comb. Traffic and wind: no comb. Drone: comb shifts with throttle. Breadboard layouts

MVP & testingDec 1

Radar station

Hardware

  • RD-03D 24 GHz radar: up to 3 moving targets, ~8 m
  • ESP32-S3 reads it on UART (GPIO17 / 18)
  • RFM95W LoRa radio, same channel as the nodes
  • USB power bank; on the roof, facing out

Software

  • Firmware: 24-byte packet, 5 per second with targets, 1 per second idle
  • Base receiver forwards it unchanged
  • ROAREE gives each target a persistent ID and fuses it with the acoustic track

Firmware compiles; parts arrive ~Nov 30. Build › Radar station
