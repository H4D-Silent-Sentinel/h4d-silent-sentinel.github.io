Counter-UAS
sensor fusion

Acoustic, radar, EO/IR (electro-optical/infrared) and RF (radio frequency) sensors fused into a common operating picture.

Beyond
line of sight

Group 1 UAS (unmanned aircraft systems) at low altitude in urban terrain are masked from radar and EO/IR by structures. Detections from all sensors feed one Kalman filter in ROAREE (Remote Observation, Autonomous Recognition, and Event Evaluation), the C2 (command and control) software on the operator's laptop. Target identity is maintained through coverage gaps. ROAREE cues sensors to the predicted position and publishes tracks to TAK (Team Awareness Kit) as CoT (Cursor on Target). Architecture

Radar detects the target. TRK-01 opens.

The target leaves radar range. TRK-01 continues on its last estimate.

Acoustic nodes at street level and on the roof detect the rotors and update TRK-01.

Radar detects the target again. Radar and acoustic detections are fused. TRK-01 keeps its identifier and history.

01

Acoustic Node

4 ICS-43434 MEMS (micro-electro-mechanical systems) microphones on an ESP32-S3. Detects rotor blade-pass harmonics. Position from relative received level across nodes. Passive.

Link
LoRa 915 MHz
Nodes
8 per building: every corner, street and roof · demo: 4 (2 corners)

Radar Node

RD-03D 24 GHz radar on an ESP32-S3 with an RFM95W radio, on the roof facing out. Range, azimuth and radial velocity of moving targets.

Link
LoRa 915 MHz via base receiver
Coverage
±60° each, 4 per building for 360° · ~8 m (RD-03D) · demo: 1

Mission Model Canvas

Key partners

Outside help the mission depends on

  • COL Nate Huston, CENTCOM (sponsor)
  • Lennart Schulze, computer vision researcher
  • Alon Simon Levin, PhD, sensor fusion
  • Yaron Zussman, sensor integration
  • Host-nation stakeholders, through the sponsor
  • Radar, camera and RF vendors: ROAREE takes their feeds

Key activities

Work the team does

  • Beneficiary interviews
  • Build and field-test acoustic nodes
  • Node firmware: rotor detection, LoRa reports
  • Fusion software: ROAREE, one track per drone
  • Integrate radar, cameras, RF, TAK
  • Chaser onboard AI on the Jetson Orin Nano

Key resources

Hardware, data, people, money

  • Acoustic nodes: ESP32-S3, 4 MEMS mics, LoRa (demo: 4)
  • RD-03D radar station
  • Pluto SDR (RF)
  • Jetson Orin Nano
  • Public drone-audio and radar-track datasets
  • Street maps: OpenStreetMap, Google Earth
  • $1,000 class budget

Value propositions

What beneficiaries gain

  • One track that holds when a sensor loses the drone
  • Cues radar, cameras and the chaser to the drone's position
  • Works with sensors bases already own
  • ~$125 per acoustic node
  • Visual tracker: ~94% of frames on target (10 held-out videos)

Buy-in and support

Who must approve or back it

  • COL Huston and CENTCOM
  • Host-nation coordination: political sensitivity is the key risk
  • Operators: tracks must appear in ATAK/TAK

Deployment

How it reaches use

  • Phase 1: acoustic nodes and fusion software at one base
  • Phase 2: existing radar, cameras, RF over CoT and SAPIENT
  • Phase 3: chaser drone with onboard AI
  • Tests: datasets, bench, then field outside NYC airspace

Beneficiaries

Who gains from it

  • C-UAS operator: primary user
  • Force protection: mission owner
  • SECFOR: response user
  • Deployed personnel: protected
  • Host / local government: external coordination

Mission budget / cost

What it costs

  • $1,000 class budget; ~$818 spent (3 orders)
  • ~$125 per acoustic node
  • Software runs on a laptop
  • No custom radar: feeds come from what bases already have

Mission achievement / impact factors

How success is measured

  • Risk: hearing rotors through city noise and echoes (multipath)
  • Next milestone: one node tells a drone from street noise, then a 4-node test
  • Success: track kept through the gap; time to reacquire when the drone reappears
  • Visual tracker tested: 0.7% false alarms, 0.15 s average reacquire
